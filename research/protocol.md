# Research Protocol — Write with AI

Adapted from [`docs/research-methodology.md`](../docs/research-methodology.md).
One page of discipline that prevents a hundred pages of regret.

## 1. Decision this research informs

Every factual statement printed in the book must be **accurate enough that a beginner
acting on it will not be misled** — about craft, AI capabilities, ethics, law, or
publishing. When in doubt: hedge, date-stamp, or cut.

## 2. Main questions

1. Which craft teachings are stable consensus vs. contested opinion? (attribute accordingly)
2. What can current AI assistants actually do for fiction writers? (test, don't assume)
3. What are the disclosure/copyright rules a beginner must know? (per jurisdiction covered)
4. Which figures are perishable and what is their re-verification date?

## 3. Scope

- **In:** fiction craft fundamentals; LLM capabilities/limits relevant to writing;
  copyright/disclosure basics (US baseline + UK/EU/BR boxes); publishing-path trade-offs.
- **Out:** exhaustive legal treatise; model benchmarks; marketing/growth tactics;
  non-fiction/business writing.

## 4. Source priority (closest-to-phenomenon first)

| Need | Go here first |
|------|---------------|
| Statute / regulation | Official text (e.g., copyright office, legislation portal) |
| Publisher/contest AI policy | The publisher's own current policy page (dated access) |
| Model capability | The provider's official docs, then your own test |
| Empirical figure (productivity etc.) | The original study; preserve units, population, date |
| Craft teaching | Established craft sources; label consensus vs. opinion |
| Market figure | Primary producer of the figure; date-stamp or cut |

## 5. Inclusion / exclusion

- **Include:** primary/official sources; original studies; named, checkable policies.
- **Exclude:** unsourced blog claims, "everybody knows," AI-generated summaries used as
  evidence, stale statistics presented as current.

## 6. Quality check (per source, 0–2 each — from the backbone methodology)

Provenance · Method · Proximity to data · Currency · Independence · Reproducibility.
Total informs judgment; it never replaces it. A current statute beats a recent blog post
even if the blog post "scores higher."

## 7. Claim workflow

1. Author flags claim in chapter `Research notes` with a `C0XX` ID.
2. Researcher fills a row in [`claim-register.md`](claim-register.md)
   (evidence, source type, confidence, date).
3. Chapter cannot move `draft → revision` with unverified factual claims.
4. **Perishable claims** (capabilities, policies, figures) get a re-verification date;
   all are re-checked in the pre-publication pass.

## 8. Counter-evidence rule

For every advisory claim ("AI feedback helps…", "indie pays better…"), record what would
change the advice. If we didn't look for the contrary case, the advice doesn't ship.

## 9. AI-use in research

AI may: decompose questions, draft search terms, summarize for triage, compare texts.
AI may not: be cited as a source, substitute for opening the primary document, or touch
personal/confidential data. Tool + task logged per project policy (feeds Appendix F).

## 10. Stop criteria (per claim batch)

Stop when: no new high-quality source changes the conclusion; new hits repeat extracted
evidence; contradictions are explained or kept as stated uncertainty; and the remaining
uncertainty wouldn't change the reader-facing advice.
