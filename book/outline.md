# Master Outline — Write with AI (working title)

**Subtitle idea:** *A Practical Manual for Beginners: From First Idea to Finished Story,
with an AI Writing Partner*

- **Audience:** beginner fiction writers (zero assumed knowledge of craft or AI)
- **Language:** English
- **Target:** 16 chapters + 6 appendices, ~115,000–125,000 words (≈ 350–400 print pages)

## Book thesis

Beginners don't need talent or permission to write fiction — they need a repeatable
process. This book teaches story fundamentals first, then pairs **each stage** of the
writing process with concrete AI-assisted techniques: prompts that work, exercises that
build skill, workflows that survive contact with a real draft, and honest guidance on
where AI helps, where it harms, and how to keep your own voice.

## Reader journey

| After… | The reader can… |
|--------|-----------------|
| Part I (Ch 1–4) | Write a complete short story **without** AI |
| Part II (Ch 5–7) | Understand what AI does, prompt it well, and run an organized project |
| Part III (Ch 8–13) | Take a story from idea → outline → draft → revision **with** AI |
| Part IV (Ch 14–16) | Protect their voice, handle ethics/disclosure, and publish |
| Appendices | Work from prompt cards, fix common failures, check terms |

## Running examples (convention)

To avoid abstract advice, the book develops **two running examples** across Part III:

- **Example A — literary/contemporary short story:** built from premise to final draft
  across Chapters 8–13 (same story, evolving).
- **Example B — genre scenes (mystery/sci-fi):** short, self-contained demonstrations
  for techniques that need a different register (dialogue, action, worldbuilding).

> Open decision: final genres and titles of the running examples (see "Open decisions").

## Front matter (~4,000 words)

- **Preface:** why this book, who it's for, what it won't do (not a "get rich with AI"
  book; not a replacement for reading widely).
- **How to Use This Book:** three reading paths (straight through / craft-first /
  AI-first), how exercises and prompt cards work, what "try it now" boxes mean.

## Part I — Foundations

### Ch 1 — Why Write Fiction with AI? (~6,000)
- **Goal:** set the mindset: AI as thought partner, not ghostwriter; the co-creation model.
- **Topics:** what AI is genuinely good at (variation, brainstorming, consistency checks,
  tireless revision help) vs. bad at (taste, lived experience, true novelty, knowing when
  to stop); the book's golden rule — *you* decide, AI proposes; overview of the workflow.
- **Worked example:** same weak premise, three AI-assisted improvements (annotated).
- **Exercises:** write 200 words unaided (baseline); compare unaided vs. AI-assisted revision.
- **Prompt cards:** none yet (deliberate — craft first).
- **Verify:** any productivity/efficiency figures cited → `research/claim-register.md`
  (C005, C006); keep units and context or cut.

### Ch 2 — Story Fundamentals: Premise, Stakes, Structure (~6,000)
- **Goal:** the reader can state a premise, raise stakes, and shape a simple structure.
- **Topics:** premise vs. idea; protagonist + goal + obstacle + stakes; three-act shape,
  Fichtean curve, and kishōtenketsu (brief, beginner-safe); beginnings/endings; the
  "one-page story skeleton."
- **Worked example:** skeleton of Example A.
- **Exercises:** 10 premises in 20 minutes; stakes ladder; skeleton for an old favorite film.
- **Verify:** craft terminology against selected craft sources (C004).

### Ch 3 — Character Fundamentals: Desire, Flaw, Arc (~6,000)
- **Goal:** the reader can build a character with desire, flaw, and a plausible arc.
- **Topics:** want vs. need; flaw and wound (without therapy-speak); flat vs. round;
  arcs (positive, negative, flat); character voice basics; the character sheet.
- **Worked example:** character sheet for Example A's protagonist.
- **Exercises:** want/need for three known characters; flaw-to-climax chain.

### Ch 4 — Setting, Scene & Point of View (~6,000)
- **Goal:** the reader can write a complete scene with a consistent POV.
- **Topics:** scene = goal + conflict + outcome; showing vs. telling (practical, not dogmatic);
  POV options (first, close third, omniscient) and psychic distance; setting as pressure;
  dialogue mechanics basics.
- **Worked example:** one scene of Example A, before/after revision (unaided).
- **Exercises:** rewrite a summary as a scene; same event in two POVs.
- **Milestone:** reader finishes Part I with a short story draft written without AI.

## Part II — The AI Toolkit

### Ch 5 — How Language Models Work: A Writer's Practical Guide (~6,500)
- **Goal:** a correct-enough mental model: prediction, context, instruction-following, limits.
- **Topics:** tokens and context windows (practical consequences); why AI is confident and
  wrong; hallucinations; knowledge cutoffs; randomness/temperature in plain terms; privacy:
  what you paste goes somewhere; model differences without brand worship.
- **Policy:** generic-first tool references; any specific capability figure is **perishable**
  and must be re-verified pre-publication (C002).
- **Exercises:** elicit a confident wrong answer; test the same prompt at different
  specificity levels.

### Ch 6 — Prompt Craft for Fiction (~6,500)
- **Goal:** the reader can write prompts that produce usable fiction material.
- **Topics:** anatomy of a fiction prompt (role, context, task, constraints, output shape);
  constraints beat adjectives; iterative prompting (draft → critique → refine); few-shot
  with your own prose; negative constraints; prompt chains for long work; saving/reusing prompts.
- **Prompt cards:** PC-01…PC-06 (first entries of Appendix A).
- **Exercises:** fix five broken prompts; build a reusable "scene drafter" prompt.

### Ch 7 — Your Writing Environment & Project Setup (~6,500)
- **Goal:** an organized project the reader can sustain for months.
- **Topics:** project folder anatomy (manuscript, bible, research, prompts, cuts);
  versioning drafts (simple numbering + changelogs); the story bible; session routines;
  word-count tracking; backups; working with AI chat history as project memory.
- **Deliverable:** reader sets up their real project using the book's template.
- **Templates:** story-bible template, session log (also used for Appendix F).

## Part III — The Workflow

### Ch 8 — Premise & Concept Development with AI (~7,000)
- **Goal:** generate, test, and select a premise worth months of work.
- **Topics:** ideation prompts (constraints, combinations, "what if" ladders); testing
  premises (stakes check, novelty check, "why me, why now"); loglines; killing darlings
  early; Example A premise development, full transcript (abridged + annotated).
- **Prompt cards:** PC-07…PC-10.

### Ch 9 — Outlining & Structure with AI (~7,000)
- **Goal:** a beat-level outline the reader trusts.
- **Topics:** outline granularity (acts → chapters → beats); AI as structure critic;
  pacing checks; subplot weaving; outline revision loops; when to abandon the outline.
- **Worked example:** Example A outline v1 → AI critique → v3 (annotated diffs).

### Ch 10 — Drafting Scenes with AI (~7,000)
- **Goal:** draft scenes faster without surrendering authorship.
- **Topics:** five drafting modes (dictate, co-draft, expand, rewrite, dialogue-first);
  scene-beat prompting; keeping tense/POV consistent; description without purple prose;
  action and dialogue; the "voice anchor" technique (your paragraph first).
- **Worked example:** Example A scene drafted three ways, compared.

### Ch 11 — Developing Characters with AI (~7,000)
- **Goal:** deeper characters, distinct voices.
- **Topics:** AI character interviews (and their limits); backstory on demand vs. on need;
  tracking arcs across chapters; dialogue voice tests; supporting cast differentiation;
  avoiding stereotype drift; Example B genre-scene demos.
- **Pitfall callout:** AI flattens characters toward the agreeable average — countermeasures.

### Ch 12 — Revision & Self-Editing with AI (~7,000)
- **Goal:** run structured revision passes instead of endless tinkering.
- **Topics:** the pass system (structure → character → scene → line → continuity);
  AI as developmental reader, line editor, continuity checker; change logs; knowing when
  a draft is done; Example A revision pass transcripts.
- **Checklists:** per-pass checklists (reused in Appendix C).

### Ch 13 — Feedback, Beta Readers & Iteration (~7,000)
- **Goal:** get useful human feedback and integrate it with AI help.
- **Topics:** what to ask beta readers (question design); AI-simulated reader perspectives
  — with explicit caveats (not a substitute for humans); triaging contradictory feedback;
  revision planning; the "feedback to changes" table.
- **Verify:** any claim about AI feedback quality must be hedged and sourced or cut.

## Part IV — Craft, Ethics & Career

### Ch 14 — Voice, Style & Originality in the Age of AI (~6,000)
- **Goal:** sound like yourself, on purpose.
- **Topics:** diagnosing "generic AI prose" (hedging, thesaurus abuse, rhythmic monotony);
  style exercises (imitation → variation → voice); sentence craft basics; originality as
  recombination + lived specificity; building a personal style sheet.
- **Exercises:** de-AI a paragraph; style imitation ladder.

### Ch 15 — Ethics, Disclosure & the Law (~6,000)
- **Goal:** publish without unpleasant surprises.
- **Topics:** plagiarism vs. inspiration with AI in the loop; disclosure norms (publishers,
  contests, platforms — policies vary, verify); copyright basics with jurisdiction boxes
  (US baseline; UK/EU/BR notes); contracts and "AI clauses"; privacy (real people as
  characters/input); academic-integrity parallels.
- **Verify:** every legal/regulatory statement → claim register (C001, C003, C007);
  re-verify at publication; "consult a lawyer" framing, not legal advice.
- **Policy:** no jurisdiction's law stated from memory — statute or official guidance only.

### Ch 16 — Publishing Paths & Your Writing Practice (~6,000)
- **Goal:** a concrete next-12-months plan.
- **Topics:** traditional vs. indie vs. serial vs. small press (trade-offs, realistic costs/
  timelines); querying basics; self-publishing checklist; building a sustainable practice;
  the 90-day plan; measuring progress; community and critique groups.
- **Verify:** market figures are perishable — verify or generalize (no stale statistics).
