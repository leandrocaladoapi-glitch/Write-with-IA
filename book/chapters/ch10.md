## Opening hook

Three writers, identical beat cards for the same scene — Mara's kitchen fight, from outline-v3: *she inventories; he refuses the lids joke; the December line lands; she retreats.* Same beats, same story, same Tuesday morning. By noon their drafts look like three different professions passed through the room.

**Writer A** dictated for forty minutes while walking, then pasted the transcript: a alive, clumsy engine — the December line *happens* in the right place, but "wrong kind of clean" arrives as "hands still dirty from working," three paragraphs of weather sneak in, and the fight's ending dissolves into dictation's natural fade. **Writer B** co-drafted: wrote her own opening paragraph first — the soup-pot image, hand-typed — then asked the assistant for the next 300 words from the beats; the seam between her paragraph and the generation shows like a zipper, but the zipper is *honest*, and half the machine's lines are already wrong in useful ways she'll fix with a red pen. **Writer C** used expand-mode: typed only the skeleton of the confrontation as six flat lines ("she says X; he counters Y; the journal is almost mentioned; she packs one box wrong on purpose") and asked for each line to be dramatized *individually*; C's draft is the most even — evenly flat, evenly safe, until C does the rewrite pass that puts the December line's actual knife-edge back in by hand.

Same beats, three drafts, three *cost structures*: A is fast and needs line surgery; B is the most *theirs* at sentence level and needs structure patience; C is consistent and needs injections of risk. The beat cards are not the variable — **the drafting mode is**. This chapter's whole argument is that "drafting with AI" is not one activity but five distinct modes with different speed/voice/control profiles, plus the two disciplines that keep any of them from erasing you: **scene-beat prompting** (locks for POV/tense/distance that make machine prose *shippable* instead of merely present) and the **voice anchor** — your paragraph first, every time, no exceptions. By the end you'll have run all five modes once (the checklist demands it), drafted a full scene with anchors and marked every machine sentence (the Stretch), and chosen modes *deliberately* per scene type for the rest of your project (the log discipline).

## Learning objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

- [ ] Draft in five modes: dictate, co-draft, expand, rewrite, dialogue-first — and say when each wins.
- [ ] Prompt from scene beats to prose (PC-14) with tense/POV/distance locked.
- [ ] Keep description sharp (PC-16) and dialogue alive (PC-15) inside a draft.
- [ ] Use the voice-anchor technique on every AI-drafted passage — and mark your machine sentences in the log.

## The five drafting modes

Each mode is a different *position on the delegation gradient* from Chapter 1 — stated as you-retain / machine-does ratios, with the scenes each one wins:

**1. Dictate (you generate everything; machine transcribes/orders).** You speak the scene aloud — preferably walking; the assistant cleans filler into readable paragraphs (a transform task, not a drafting task — model's job: *your* spoken prose, tightened). *Wins when:* you know the scene and need speed past self-censorship; your spoken voice is *better* than your typed voice (common: speech bypasses the inner editor that hedges on the keyboard); fight scenes, confession scenes, rants. *Cost:* structure drift and dictation's soft endings (hook's Writer A) — the pass after dictation is always *structural*, rarely line-level, because the sentences are already yours. *Retain ratio:* you 100% of content, machine ~5% of words (filler trims only).

**2. Co-draft (anchor first, then generate the middle).** Your opening paragraph (or first line + beat notes) typed by hand — the *voice anchor* — then PC-01 asks for continuation under locks; you alternate: anchor a paragraph → generate two → hand-write the next one. *Wins when:* you need the machine's throughput *in your voice's neighborhood* and you can write faster than you can edit (some days it's inverted — see rewrite). The chapter's workhorse mode; the zipper-seam of Writer B is the *feature*: seams mark where your prose last touched, and Chapter 14's diagnostics hunt seams that *never* got touched. *Retain ratio:* target 40–60% machine-origin sentences surviving to draft — *with* your paragraph-anchors between them (above ~70% survival, the anchor discipline has slipped: see pitfall).

**3. Expand (you write skeleton-lines; machine dramatizes them).** Flat six-line confrontation (Writer C) → PC-14 expand mode per line or per pair of lines. *Wins when:* the *structure* is certain and prose is the bottleneck; heavily-beaten chapters straight from outline-v3; when your own drafting energy is low but judgment is high (expansion-review is judgment work — a good mode for tired afternoons that still counts as drafting hours). *Cost:* evenness — expansion inherits the skeleton's temperature, so flat lines yield flat prose *faithfully*; the cure is writing *charged* skeleton lines (the Ch 9 line clinic applied to your own level-3 beats: "the lids joke lands wrong; she almost says mother's name" expands better than "they argue about boxes"). *Retain ratio:* similar to co-draft, but your content decisions ride in the skeleton instead of the anchor.

**4. Rewrite (machine transforms your full draft).** Your scene exists, awkward but yours → targeted transforms: "tighten to 70% length keeping POV lock and the line『…』 untouched"; "cut every adverb that modifies a dialogue tag"; "make the physical business continuous — she's packing throughout." *Wins when:* you draft long and cut better (the classic draft-fat → revision-lean path, compressed); when a scene's *events* are right but its *surface* is flabby. *Danger specific to this mode:* transformation silently *re-voices* — every rewrite pass drifts toward the model's house register (Ch 5, dial 3) — which is why rewrite-mode output passes through the voice anchor *in reverse*: diff it against your version and *keep your diction wherever the machine merely prettied* (the de-AI pass of Ch 14 previewed; in drafting, run the diff lightly, at sentence level, now). *Retain ratio:* machine touches most sentences; **your version remains the fallback** — never overwrite draft-0N with transformed text without a mint + the original addressable (the lost-draft hook's other mouth: a transform *is* a revision, versioned like one).

**5. Dialogue-first (words before world).** The scene starts as *pure dialogue*, unattributed, no action beats — machine or you, alternating speakers from the beat cards; *then* pass two adds attribution, action beats, and physical continuity; pass three (yours, always) does subtext and voice-differentiation per Chapter 3's field 10 and Chapter 11's voice tests. *Wins when:* relationship is the scene (the kitchen fight qualifies — hook's alternative path), when your narration tends to *avoid* the confrontation (dialogue-first forces contact), when testing character voices (Ch 11's demos run this mode). *Cost:* unattributed dialogue invites speaker-collapse (two characters finishing each other's *sentences in the same rhythm* — F-02's birth) — the pass-two attribution step is where you catch it, reading speeches *blind* (tags covered) as Chapter 4's mechanics demand. *Retain ratio:* content yours and machine's in alternation; the *selection of who says what* must be yours from the start (speaker choice *is* characterization — never delegate it, even in draft).

**Mode selection, systematized.** Write this table into your story bible or the top of your session log — and *log the mode per scene* (Ch 7's Mode line), because Ch 14's audit reads that column:

| Scene type | Default mode | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Confrontation / high-emotion | Dialogue-first or dictate | contact and heat; narration would flinch |
| Arrival / orientation | Dictate (walk it) | your perception, transcribed |
| Beaten structure, known turns | Expand | structure certain; prose is the bottleneck |
| Middle-of-flow continuation | Co-draft with anchors | voice proximity + throughput |
| Scene you've written flabby | Rewrite (transform) | events right, surface wrong |
| Discovery-critical scene (premise-multiplying) | Hand-write first, *then* any mode | the story is *deciding something* — no delegation of decisions |

That last row is the safety rail in table form: **modes serve scenes where the story's decisions are already made** (beats, stakes, outcomes — Chapters 2/3/9's outputs). When a scene will *discover* new story — the midpoint that changes the map, the line that reveals what a character actually wants — hand-write it first, unassisted, then let any mode polish the remainder. Discovery behind the machine's suggestions is how premises drift silently (the homogenization risk Ch 8 flagged, arriving at scene scale): the tool completes probable continuations, and your story's *un*probable turn is exactly what probabilistic completion flattens first.

> TRY IT — Mode sampler: take one beat from your own outline and draft it *twice*, five minutes each: (a) dictate (phone voice memo is fine — transcribe roughly by hand), (b) expand (charged skeleton line → PC-14). Compare not quality but *feel*: which needed less courage to start? which sentence-level touched more of yours? Write two lines in the session log — that's your personal mode-preference baseline, to re-check after three scenes.

## Scene-beat prompting

Modes all run on the same engine: **beats → prose under locks**. The card that makes machine prose *shippable* rather than merely present:

> PROMPT CARD PC-14 — Scene expander (beats → prose)
>
> ```text
> <!-- PC-14 v1 · tested 2026-09 · generic, no model-specific syntax -->
> Role: drafting apprentice; output is material for my red pen.
> Context (bible pack): [relevant names/spellings, story rules, the
> scene's stakes line] + ANCHOR (my prose, voice target — match
> rhythm/diction, do not improve):
> [your hand-written paragraph]
> Beats (change-lines, in order — dramatize in this order, no new
> plot turns):
> 1. [...]
> LOCKS (violate none): POV [close third, Mara only]; tense [past];
> distance [2–4 on the ladder, deepest only at the outcome]; length
> [350–480 words]; no backstory paragraphs; banned words: [from
> bible §5 / style sheet]; dialogue carries subtext — no character
> states the scene's conflict outright; END BEAT [#] must land as
> [action / refusal / unresolved question], no summary sentence
> after it.
> Output: prose only after "SCENE:" — no titles, no notes, no
> explanation of choices.
> ```

**Why locks are load-bearing, one per line.** *POV + anchor-pasted:* the single most common machine-draft failure is distance drift (deep interior flashbulbs in line 2, camera-wide in line 4 — F-04's birth); naming the anchor *range* ("2–4, deepest only at outcome") gives the critic pass a checkable contract, not a vibe. *Tense:* untensed prompts produce mid-scene shifts within one paragraph — lock it adjacent to the task, every time (Chapter 5's nearness rule for the most-violated lock). *Banned words from bible §5/style sheet:* the constraint packs from Chapter 6 at project altitude — the draft inherits your story's *refusals*, not just its facts. *No character states the conflict outright:* subtext-as-constraint — the most common way drafts sound like AI is characters *explaining the scene to each other*; forbid it structurally and the dialogue must *do* work instead. *End-beat shape with no trailing summary:* the Ch 4 scene-ending discipline enforced prompt-side (the model's natural ending is a reflective cadence — the trailing summary is almost its signature move; the explicit end-shape plus "no summary after" closes the door). *Bible pack before beats:* continuity context *first*, so the desk's opening frames the generation (names spelled right, rules in view) — bible-first prompting (Ch 7) at the point of use.

**Beat discipline on your side:** beats are *change-lines* (Ch 9's clinic — not "they argue" but "the lids joke lands wrong; she almost says mother's name"); beats *include the outcome* (never ask the machine to *find* the scene's turn — you found it in outlining; it dramatizes your finding); and beats come from the *session's* level-3 work (per-session, not pre-mined — the freeze-line rule). Two locks worth adding for specific scenes: **speech-length lock** ("dialogue lines ≤ N words") when drafting with a character whose voice is clipped (bible §2's voice notes → prompt locks — the sheet *pays* here), and **quantity lock on interiority** ("max 2 interior summary sentences per 100 words") when your POV character's anxiety tempts purple spirals. Locks you add because *this scene's history* demands them are how the card stays alive across projects — the knobs Appendix A lists per card.

## Description and dialogue

Two craft surfaces the machine touches constantly, each needing its instrument:

**Description sharpener (PC-16) — run *after* a draft exists, never as the drafting frame.** Ask the machine to *diagnose and replace at constant count*, not to decorate:

```text
<!-- PC-16 v1 · tested 2026-09 · generic -->
Task: DESCRIPTION PASS on the scene below — diagnostic first.
1. List every descriptive sentence. Tag each [owned] (could only be
   in this story — someone's fingerprint on it) or [generic] (any
   story's kitchen/harbor/room — Ch 4's ladder rung 2).
2. For each [generic]: propose ONE replacement at the SAME length,
   spending: this scene's pressure (what's just happened), one
   sensory channel not yet used in the paragraph, and no new
   information the scene hasn't learned yet.
3. Keep [owned] untouched, verbatim.
Constraints: total descriptive word count may not increase; no
weather indoors unless storm-rule bible §5 applies; no similes
drawn from: velvet, shroud, grave, ocean of.
Output: tagged list, then the revised scene, then a count diff.
```

Constant-count replacement is the whole trick: description passes that allow *growth* converge on purple (adjective accretion is the model's easiest motion — F-01's engine); forcing same-length swaps with owned-untouched makes the pass *only* able to spend your story's specificity where the generic sat — the Ch 4 concrete-detail ladder, executed by contract. The banned-simile list is your style sheet's growing edge (Ch 14 formalizes it — every cliché the de-AI pass kills graduates onto a lock list like this).

**Dialogue-first drafter (PC-15) — the contact instrument:**

```text
<!-- PC-15 v1 · tested 2026-09 · generic -->
Role: unattributed dialogue generator; speakers distinguished ONLY
by their speech, never by my telling you who talks.
Context: two speakers' VOICE NOTES (from character sheets — diction,
syntax, verbal habit, never-say) + situation + beats 1–N.
Task: draft the exchange with NO dialogue tags and NO action beats —
just alternating lines, each speaker marked only as A:/B:.
Locks: each line advances, refuses, or deflects — no greetings, no
small talk openings; every A-line must be *unlike* a line B could
say (swap test: if a line works for either speaker, rewrite it);
the scene's conflict never stated in plain words; end on B's
non-answer.
Then STOP — attribution pass is mine.
Output: A:/B: lines only.
```

The unattributed format is diagnostic *by construction*: with no tags to hide behind, speaker-collapse (everybody sounds like the same helpful intern — Ch 11's flattening problem) becomes *visible as rhythm similarity*, and the swap test gives you the falsification check Chapter 11 will formalize (blind attribution). The voice notes in context are the sheets' field-10 payoff: diction/syntax/habit constraints *per speaker* are what make A and B diverge before you've written a single action beat. **The attribution pass stays yours** — who gets interrupted, who doesn't pick up the tea, who lies with a gesture: physical business under dialogue is where *relationship* gets staged (Ch 4's mechanics), and it is the last thing on this project you should ever automate.

**Action beats in dialogue:** two mechanical rules that fix most machine-written (and beginner-written) exchanges: **beats must do** (an action beat is a *small decision or state* — packing, not turning — not a random gesture inserted between lines to break up the white space; if the beat could be deleted without the scene noticing, it's gesture-confetti); and **one beat's information per line** (the beat carries what words don't — his "wrong kind of clean" *said* the work he'd been doing; dialogue said the rest — no duplication). Run PC-15 output through both rules in the attribution pass and the exchange gains a body without gaining fat.

## The voice anchor

The chapter's central discipline, stated as a law: **in every AI-drafted scene, you write the first paragraph — and at minimum every third paragraph thereafter — by hand, before any generation touches that region of the draft.**

The anchor does three jobs simultaneously. **Voice calibration:** your hand-typed paragraph sits *on the desk* as few-shot data (Ch 6's PC-04 at scene scale) — the generation that follows matches *your* recent rhythm, not the house register; each anchor you write refreshes the target (anchors age: a paragraph from scene 1 is stale voice-data by scene 20 — re-anchor per scene, always). **Structural presence:** anchor points fall where the scene's *decisions* live (your paragraph = the outcome's paragraph, the revelation's landing) — generation flows *between* your decision-posts, so the prose's load-bearing joints are *yours by construction*; when the Stretch asks you to "mark every AI sentence," the anchor layout means the unmarked sentences *cluster at the turns* — the draft reads as yours at its hinges even where the machine filled the straights. **Erosion early-warning:** the anchor ratio is a measurable discipline — if you catch yourself skipping anchors "just this chapter," the ratio is drifting *and so is the voice* (Ch 7's voice-drift audit uses exactly this behavior as its metric; Ch 14 repairs what slippage lets in — F-09's prevention chain starts here).

Implementation patterns, because laws need logistics: **front-anchor** (your opening paragraph, then generate — default); **interleaved** (write → generate → write → generate — for long scenes; your every-third lands on beats you shouldn't delegate); **sandwich** (your before-paragraph *and* your intended closing line exist first; generation fills between — the strongest form for scenes with known endings, as every outlined scene is: *you already wrote the last line's decision in the beats*, so type it); and **retro-anchor** for dictation-mode days (the transcript is entirely yours — anchors moot; the *rewrite* pass afterward gets anchored instead: your typed cleanup of paragraph 1 before transform-mode touches the rest). The common error to expect from honest self-audit: **anchor-as-decoration** — a token first sentence, hand-typed, with the *intent* outsourced in the very next line (you typed "The kitchen ticked" and then prompted "continue with the confrontation" *without beats* — the anchor's presence but not its function). The functional test: *would the generation be lost if your anchor paragraph vanished?* If no — the anchor was a ritual, not a calibration; re-do the scene's generation with beats + locks the card requires.

### The drafting session, timed end to end

Modes and locks become real only inside an actual hour — so here is one co-drafting session for outline-v3's beat 5, timed honestly, with the discipline points marked. (Total: 78 minutes — typical for a 500-word scene day; the *next* beat's session runs shorter as locks and voice warm.)

**[0–8 min] Orientation and pack.** Re-read draft-current's last 500 words (Ch 7's ritual — voice calibration by *reading your own recent prose* before any prompt exists); open `outline-v3`, copy beat 5's change-line plus its neighbors' pressure ("after 4's ferry-cancelled clock; before 6's discovery"); assemble the context pack from the bible (names list line, §5 rules relevant to indoor storm scenes, stakes sentence); check the style sheet's banned list for freshness. Nothing generated yet — *the desk is being set by hand*, which is the session's actual first authorship act.

**[8–15 min] Level-3 beats + anchor, hand-typed.** Write the six charged skeleton lines (the ones expand-mode will later obey — charge them *now*, Ch 9's clinic: "lids joke: expected, misses" not "they argue"); then type the anchor paragraph — the soup-pot opening, possibly re-typed fresh rather than copied (re-typing your best old paragraph is voice-recall exercise *and* anchor refresh; copying it is fine too, but the fresh typing primes rhythm better — personal preference, logged either way). Decide the mode in one line: "co-draft, interleaved, anchors at 1/4/7."

**[15–30 min] Generation leg 1.** PC-14 assembled (pack → anchor → beats → locks, end-shape for beat 5's non-answer landing) → run → *compliance grade with a pen before taste* (locks checked in order: POV range ✓ tense ✓ length ✓ no stated conflict ✓ banned-constructions class ✓ end-shape ✓) → fix violations by *prompt edit*, one variable (B-audit's guilt-slip teaches the list grows: "emotion-as-weather" joins banned *constructions*) → re-run → accept into `wip` file. Roughly three runs; typically run 3 is gradeable.

**[30–40 min] Anchor paragraph 2 (hand) + generation leg 2.** Your paragraph at the hinge (the December line's landing — *your* decision-post exactly where the scene turns) → one generation for the fallout stretch under the same locks → grade → accept. The machine's job here is *straights between posts*, and the log's mark-as-you-go discipline starts: every accepted machine paragraph gets a margin tag (`M`) *at acceptance time*, not at Stretch time — the marking tax paid incrementally costs seconds; paid at audit costs an evening.

**[40–55 min] Close the scene by hand.** The end-beat (cursor-typed-not-sent / the deleted KITCHEN) is *yours regardless of mode* — even though the beats named its shape: typing the landing yourself is the sandwich-anchor's innermost layer, and the model's reflective-cadence pull is strongest exactly at endings (why the lock exists) — do not fight it with a prompt; do it with your hands. Read the full scene cold (stand up; the 20-second reset matters — you're now a first reader, briefly).

**[55–65 min] Light pass, no transforms.** Cut double shipwrecks, duplicate images within the scene (the audit catch pattern), one adjective per clause of purple, confirm Oracle-check clean (Ch 4's audit — machine paragraphs are where unknown-knowledge slips breed). *No rewrite-mode transform yet* — transforms are Chapter 12's line-pass territory with full style-sheet context; mid-session transforms invite drift (house register accumulates while you're too inside to hear it).

**[65–78 min] Close ritual, paid in full.** Words counted (session-log block); mode logged (`co-draft`); adjudications rows if anything substantive (composite candidates, lock-v2 additions); beat folded: "scene-5 drafted → wip" + next-step line ("beat 6 is discovery-adjacent (journal found) — mode: hand-first per safety rail, then expand"); pack components filed back (bible untouched this session — note it: "no syncs needed" is still a sync *decision*); one decisions row for the anchor-function self-check ("anchors functional — generation would lose the hinges without them: yes").

That last row — the *functional anchor test* written as a habit — is the session's keystone, and the following section unpacks why that test, not word-count productivity, is this chapter's real metric.

### Reading your mode log: patterns the table can't teach

Six scenes into the discipline, the Mode column stops being bookkeeping and starts being *evidence about how you actually write* — a dataset only possible *because* the logging rule existed before the data mattered. What the patterns look like when they emerge (four composites from realistic logs — yours will have its own shapes):

**Pattern 1 — the fatigue signature.** Mode rows drift *predictably* across the week: Monday's scenes `hand-first`, Wednesday's `co-draft`, Friday's entries reading `co-draft (anchors thin — note)` three weeks running. Read that last cell honestly and the log has found something the mirror can't: *your anchor discipline degrades on a schedule*, which means F-09's prevention shouldn't be a daily willpower project — it should be **Friday's session design** (Friday = rewrite-mode on Wednesday's output instead of fresh co-drafting: judgment-heavy, generation-light — matching the instrument's reading of your capacity, not your aspiration's). The pattern's deeper lesson: *modes are matched to energy states*, and the log is the only place the mapping gets discovered — "I co-draft well tired" is either true for you or a story you're telling at 11 p.m.; the accept-rate-per-mode column (Ch 14's audit cross-ref) settles it with adjudication data: if Friday co-drafts show 70%+ acceptance, tired-you has been *outsourcing taste* — schedule differently; if they show 35% (skeptical, picky, *exactly* Ch 1's gate at full strength), the mode is genuinely energy-appropriate and the thin anchors are a *fixable habit within* an honest mode choice (anchor reminders taped to the session template, not a career crisis).

**Pattern 2 — the mode monoculture.** Eight scenes, seven `co-draft`, one `dictate`, zero `expand`/`dialogue-first`/`rewrite` — the mode table exists and *isn't being consulted*, usually because sessions open with momentum ("just keep going in yesterday's mode") rather than a two-second look at *what this scene needs* (confrontation? the table says dialogue-first; beats certain? expand; flabby prose? rewrite — the pre-choice rule from the checklist, evidently not yet habitual). The fix is embarrassingly small and disproportionately effective: **the mode decision joins the beat-list ritual** (the night-before prep now ends with "mode: ___" written beside the beat list — the decision made *while fresh*, executed *tired*, immune to momentum's default) — and one calendar-scheduled *mode-experiment scene* per fortnight (rotate the unused modes through real material until the monoculture breaks or *deliberately* narrows: if experiments confirm co-draft's dominance for your temperament, that's now a *chosen* monoculture with evidence, logged as such — the distinction between *default* and *decision* being, at this book's scale, roughly the distance between drift and voice).

**Pattern 3 — the composite-creep.** Accept-rates per mode creeping upward across months (34% → 51% → 68%) without a matching rise in *unaided-baseline* quality (the quarterly Ch 14 comparison holding flat) — the drift signature's subtle cousin: not the *prose* flattening (the audits would catch that) but the *gate* softening — you're agreeing with the machine more because the machine's suggestions are *fine* and your reviewing energy is *finite*, a slow conversion from "propose → adjudicate" toward "propose → nod." The log's pattern-spot is early precisely because *rows record decisions, not vibes*: 68% acceptance isn't a feeling you can rationalize mid-session; it's a number in your own column that the weekly audit *puts in front of you*, next to the flat baseline line, with one question the whole book has trained you to answer: *is this convergence toward quality, or toward convenience?* (The disambiguating drill, when the pattern trips: take one high-acceptance chapter and *re-adjudicate ten accepted rows blind* — cover the machine's wording, re-read your reasons. If the rows still earn their keeps, the accept-rate reflects a *calibrated* gate meeting better material — legitimate, log the finding; if half the reasons now read "was fine," the gate's been open — schedule a PC-21 re-run on that chapter with the *fresh* style sheet and write the cause-row that got you here: fatigue, monoculture, or creep *itself* — the log caught the catcher, which is what instrumentation *means*.)

**Pattern 4 — the mode-that-earned-its-keep.** The mirror image worth logging with equal seriousness: `expand` rows showing *consistently high* post-pass surgery (every expanded scene needing line-pass work in the *same place* — flat skeleton-lines rendered flatly) — the instrument indicting *your beat-writing*, not the mode: the fix lands upstream (Ch 9's charge-drills applied to *tonight's* level-3 beats: "lids joke: expected, misses" energy, not "they argue about boxes"), and the mode's rating *recovers* in the log when the inputs improve (three weeks later: expand-scenes' surgery-counts dropping — the pattern closed with a *before/after pair in the same column*, the most satisfying rows a log ever produces). Every pattern shares one architecture: **the column you kept for accountability turns out to be measuring a *system*** (energy, decisions, gates, inputs — never just "what mode I used"), and the corrections are always *design* corrections — session templates, ritual additions, scheduling changes — rather than character improvements, because *the log already proved the character was showing up*; what needed redesigning was the room the character writes in. Modes serve scenes; logs serve the writer serving scenes; and after six scenes, the table in your bible is no longer the book's — it's *yours*, with your patterns printed in it, waiting for next Sunday's audit to turn them into next week's session design.

Eight rows teaching the composite-discipline's five laws, each reason-line a specimen: **(1) verdicts attach to *text+symptom*, never to *mode-brand*** (row 8's tax-line falls whether it arrived by dictation or hand — "the machine's line" and "my line" are *not* verdict-vocabulary; "hedge-creep," "swap-violation," "fingerprint-hit" *are* — the golden rule's ledger-symmetry at row-scale: *sources are metadata; symptoms are findings*); **(2) KEEP-as-is requires an *unaided-ownership* check** (row 3's reason-line models the question — "would I have written this?" asked *honestly, once* — NOT "does this sound good" (that's the sycophancy-structure pointed at yourself: your own tolerance for machine-flattery runs through the *same* review-energy the weekly adjudication-audit measures — which is why row 3's check is *written as a test with a pass-condition*, not a mood: **ownership-claims get cross-examined like any other claim** — the sentence "I'd have written this" earns its KEEP only when you can *name the craft-reason* (fingerprint verb-class), not when it merely *feels* like yours — feeling-like-yours is exactly what good co-drafting is *designed* to produce, the whole reason the blind-shuffle exists upstream)); **(3) mode-known-failures route to *upstream* fixes, not line-level mercy** (row 6: the expand-flat signature's *mechanism* named, the *repair* sent to tomorrow's beat-list charge-drill — the repair *at the altitude that caused it*: line-replacing here would treat the symptom while the *pipeline* keeps spitting flat lines — the mode-log's pattern-column converting one session's rejection into a *process* row, the same one-way door as always: **findings that recur must graduate from text to training**); **(4) discoveries-waive-plans with written reasons** (row 5's tag-addition: the end-shape plan lost to what the scene *became* — the two-rule stand's drafting-half honored inside the merge itself — the waiver row existing so pass-5's tag-check doesn't "correct" the discovery later: *waiver-paste discipline at composite-scale*, preventing the rat-chase that fixes-what-was-already-adjudicated into its opposite); **(5) altitude-queueing beats fixing-while-here** (row 7's line-queue: the dictation-rhythm issue *belongs to pass 4*, logged with its PC-21 citation — the temptation it refuses being real (the line *bugs you now* — fixing it now *feels* efficient and *is* the F-07 entry-condition violation the pass system's whole architecture exists to prevent: **the composite's rows end the session where the *session* ends — queue, don't touch — the document's next mint reflects kept-rows only, and tomorrow's altitude reads its own list**). The eight-row block's summary number: 2 kept-as-is / 2 partial-keep / 2 dropped / 1 queue / 1 waived — *no verdicts skipped, every reason one line, zero mode-loyalties* — a merge session's output that reads exactly like any other adjudication-ritual's output *by design*: multi-mode work was supposed to feel *stranger* than this (three sources, one scene, hand-wrestling quips from an eager assistant) — the strangeness *fully absorbed* by rows, because **the ritual was always cross-altitude: now it's cross-mode too, and the verbs didn't change** — keep, reject, queue, waive, cite-the-line: the vocabulary that makes *any* text yours, however it arrived at the table.

## Worked example: Example A's scene drafted three ways

Outline-v3's beat 5 (the kitchen fight) through the three hook-paths, properly — locks identical (close third Mara, past, distance 2–4 deepest at outcome, no stated conflict, end on action/refusal, banned: *lonely, closure*), anchor paragraph identical across B and C (the soup-pot image — your Ch 4 scene's DNA), adjudicated after.

**A — Dictate transcript (transcribed, lightly trimmed; your words + filler-trim):**

"A spreadsheet is how things get done." "Yeah. Done." He puts it down wrong on the box. Wrong on purpose. "She asked for you in December, Mara. Thought you should know before you inventory the pots." [...]

*Diagnosis:* alive — "like a fish" is *spoken-image* good (dictate's prize: the inner editor didn't veto it); the *intended* structure intact through the December line; **but**: opening's goal-beat ("she sets bag down, inventory-first") vanished — dictation skipped the *stage direction* because you didn't say it aloud; ending (per full transcript) fades into a weather remark — the classic soft landing. *Fix path:* structure pass — restore goal-beat by hand (1 paragraph), cut the weather exit, land the locked end-action. Line surgery: nearly none; sentences were already yours.

**B — Co-draft (your anchor → PC-14 continuation, abridged):**

*Diagnosis:* the anchor held — machine's rhythm below it stays *close* (note "the way she always produced phone at funerals and fires" — that's anchor-regenerated *your*-idiom, the few-shot paying; though audit catch: "like a shipwreck's" also appears in *her* Ch 4 — machine **reused your own image within one paragraph** — cute, and a continuity duplicate: two shipwrecks in one story is one shipwreck too many; hand-cut the second). Structure: beats honored, end-action landed, **one banned-register slip caught and struck** ("old guilt rise…like tide" — the model's reflective cadence forcing feeling-weather *in violation of no-stated-conflict* — a lock the *critic pass* will also catch; caught earlier because the Stretch's marking discipline ran *during* drafting, not after). Seam health: anchors at paragraphs 1 and (per interleaved run) 4, 7 — hinges covered.

December: asks-for-you; pots-pricing lands. 5. cursor-animal: KITCHEN typed not sent. 6. his actual question: "Are you staying tonight?" — she counts rooms instead of answering.

*Diagnosis:* evenness both ways — the *skeleton's* charge carried ("Friday might yet be claimed" — expand faithfully rendering *your* flat line's hidden ache: the mode where your beats do the work, machine does the typing), and the **danger made visible**: lines you wrote *flat* ("cursor-animal") come back *flat*, faithfully — expand never adds risk, it *inherits* yours; note also line 6 (staying-tonight question) — a **skeleton-invented beat not in outline-v3**: expand-mode *obeyed the skeleton you typed this session* over the frozen outline (desk pressure, Ch 5 force 3: the fresher document wins) — caught because the audit diffs against `outline-v3` per beat; adjudication: the staying-question is *better* than v3's silent beat-5 ending → route it properly (v4 mint, changelog: "beat 5 outcome: added staying-question non-answer — discovered during expand-mode; kept") — divergence *documented*, never silent (Ch 9's two-rule stand in live action).

## Exercises

- **Try (15 min):** Draft one beat from your outline *three ways* in five-minute sprints: expand (charged line → PC-14), rewrite (hand-write a 120-word flat version first → transform with locks), dialogue-first (unattributed A:/B: → two minutes of your own attribution). No quality grading — mark in the margins: which start was hardest (courage), which surface touched most of your sentences (voice), which mode wants to be used again. Three lines in the session log; your mode table from theory now has *personal* rows.

- **Stretch (1–2 h):** Draft a full scene (400–700 words) with the interleaved anchor pattern — your paragraphs hand-typed at anchors 1, 4, 7 (or every third), generation under PC-14 locks between — then run **the marking discipline**: print/export, highlight every sentence that originated with the machine (use your session notes as evidence; where uncertain, *mark it as machine* — doubt = machine, by the audit's conservative rule). Count the ratio; write five lines: what percent, where the unmarked sentences cluster (they should cluster at turns — if they cluster nowhere in particular, anchors were decorative: re-generate), and one sentence on what the marked region *cost* the scene (the gap between what you'd have written and what sits there — that gap is Ch 14's de-AI brief, now with a body count).

- **Project (ongoing):** Draft your chapters under the mode discipline: the mode table lives in the bible; **every scene's mode logged** (Ch 7's Mode line, one word); every composite/adjudication gets its decisions row; discovery-critical scenes hand-drafted first, no exceptions (safety rail). Milestone to feel for: after ~six scenes, you should be choosing modes *before* opening the chat — "this is a rewrite day / this is a dialogue-first scene" — rather than falling into whatever's open. ## Pitfalls

> PITFALL — **Purple drift / POV drift (F-01 + F-04, the double helix).** *Symptom:* machine-drafted passages arrive over-described in the house register (weather inside, bodies narrating feelings, one simile per clause — *purple drift*) and/or mid-paragraph the camera jumps to another head or the tense slips present-then-past (*POV drift*); often *both at once*, because both are the model's defaults when locks are soft: reflective cadence invites both interiority-spree and sensation-spree. *Why it happens:* under-specified locks (the prompt said "close third" but not the *anchor range*; banned *words* but not banned *constructions* — B-audit's guilt-slip proves word-lists don't catch "she felt the old X rise"), the desk's context pulling toward ornate passages pasted "for voice," and drafts written past the freeze-line's discipline (no beats → model improvises turns → improvisation defaults to center, center is purple-adjacent). *Fix (layered, in drafting order):* (1) **prevent prompt-side** — PC-14 locks with anchor *range*, explicit end-shape, banned constructions *class* ("no emotion-as-weather," "no body-narrates-feeling," "max 1 figure of speech per 100 words"), conflict-never-stated, bible pack in context; (2) **catch marking-side** — the Stretch's highlight discipline run *per scene*, not per draft (doubt = machine; clusters-at-turns check); (3) **repair pass-side** — PC-16 for description at constant count, Ch 12's line pass with your style sheet's banned list for the rest; (4) **audit-side** — Ch 7's monthly voice-drift check quantifies whether the pattern is *drifting up* across sessions (if yes: anchors aged or skipped — return to the functional anchor test). One systemic note: purple and POV slips are *lock gaps*, not model sins — the card's job is to make the contract violation *checkable* by a critic that reads locks as its scoring rubric (PC-02's five questions + the locks list pasted = the critic grades compliance *first*, which is why the rubric lives in the prompt, not in your head).

Related failures for Appendix C: **F-01 (purple/generic description — PC-16's home)**, **F-04 (POV/tense drift — locks + marking + Ch 12 audit)**; both feed F-09 (voice erosion) when unaddressed across many scenes — the prevention chain's order: locks → marking → passes → audit, *every layer cheaper than the next*.

## Key takeaways

- **"Drafting with AI" is five modes with different cost structures** — dictate (heat, needs structure surgery), co-draft (voice-proximity + throughput), expand (obeys your skeleton — flat in, flat out), rewrite (events-right/surface-wrong, drifts to house register), dialogue-first (contact; attribution pass stays yours). Choose from the mode table *before* opening the chat; log the mode per scene.
- **Scene-beat prompting makes machine prose shippable:** bible pack + your anchor + change-line beats + *locks* (POV anchor-range, tense near generation, banned constructions not just words, no stated conflict, explicit end-shape) — the contract is what the critic grades, so write it into PC-14 where it's checkable.
- **Description and dialogue get instruments, not vibes:** PC-16's constant-count owned-versus-generic replacement prevents purple inflation; PC-15's unattributed format makes speaker-collapse visible; attribution and physical business stay yours — relationship is staged in the beats between words.
- **The voice anchor is the authorship law of this chapter:** your paragraph first, interleaved thereafter, refreshed per scene — calibration data, structural hinges, and erosion meter in one habit; decorative anchors fail the functional test.
- **Discovery-critical scenes are hand-drafted first, always** — the safety rail under everything above: modes serve *made* decisions; the story's *un*probable turns get your unassisted hands, then any mode may polish around them. The real speed gain isn't faster prose — it's affording three ways to see one scene before choosing, adjudicated in your file, converted into composites.
