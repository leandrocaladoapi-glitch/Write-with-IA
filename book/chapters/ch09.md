## Opening hook

The outline looked *fine*. Forty-one beats, arrows between them, a color for each act — you remember the afternoon you made it, the satisfaction of a machine assembled. Then you read it aloud in order, the way you'd read a stranger's, and somewhere around beat 19 the air went out: three acts of *things happening* between your two good scenes, nothing anyone wants spilling over into anything else, no reason the middle shouldn't be skipped. The Act II hole — not a missing event (there are events; look at all the events) but a missing *pressure*: no rung movement, no map change, just a corridor your protagonist walks while the outline congratulates itself on arrows.

Here's the part that stings: the outline had *felt* fine for six weeks. Feeling is a terrible structure instrument — you cannot feel a pacing hole in a document you wrote in the dopamine of invention; you can only *test* for it. What found it in the end took twenty minutes: a structured critique pass where the machine was ordered to hunt exactly this failure — "where does pressure flatline for more than two beats? where does the protagonist stop *wanting on-page*?" — and then *you* adjudicating its findings against the skeleton's stakes sentence, keeping three, rejecting two, rebuilding beats 19–26 by hand from the kept ones.

This chapter installs that testing apparatus. Outlining with AI means three things, and only three: **granularity control** (acts → chapters → beats, at the resolution your story actually needs), **the machine as structure critic** (PC-11 asks for problems, never praise — you adjudicate everything in writing), and **pacing and subplot instruments** (PC-12, PC-13) that catch what eyes miss. Plus the escape hatch the chapter must keep honest: *when to abandon the outline*, because an outline serves the draft, not the other way around (the rule that also, quietly, keeps discovery writers in this book at all). By the end, Example A will have gone through the full v1 → critique → v3 loop in front of you, annotated, and your own beat outline will exist — frozen, with all later changes routed through the changelog.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

- [ ] Expand a skeleton into a beat-level outline (acts → chapters → beats) at the right resolution.
- [ ] Use AI as a structure critic without surrendering decisions (PC-11, adjudicated).
- [ ] Check pacing and weave subplots deliberately (PC-12, PC-13).
- [ ] Revise an outline through versions (v1 → v3) and know when to abandon it instead.

## Outline granularity

**Granularity** is the outline's zoom level: too coarse and it can't guide scenes; too fine and it freezes accidents that wanted to be better. The three-level ladder, with beginner-sized guidance for each:

**Level 1 — Acts/skeleton-derived movements (3–6 entries).** You already own this: Chapter 2's skeleton *is* a level-1 outline (disturbance, point of no return, midpoint/crisis, climax preview, ending preview — five beats with causes). Never re-outline at this level from scratch; expand *from* the skeleton, which is why it lives in the bible. Rule of thumb: if a skeleton field is soft (Chapter 2's diagnosis guide), no amount of beat-level detail below it will compensate — fix upward first.

**Level 2 — Chapters/sequence beats (one line each, 12–40 for a novel; 5–12 for a long short story).** The workhorse level for Part III. Each entry: *what changes* in one line, stated as change, not activity — "Mara arrives; keys + notary letter; wants out by dark" (three changes compressed) beats "Mara's arrival scene" (an activity label with no opinion about pressure). The line-test for a level-2 beat: **does it contain a want, a cost, or a map-change?** If it contains none, it's scenery filed as structure — demote it to a scene detail inside a neighboring beat or cut it. Chapter granularity should also answer *ordering* questions: every beat states enough of its neighbors' relationship ("before the fight / after the journal") that POV chapters and time skips stay legible in the list itself.

**Level 3 — Scene beats inside a chapter (3–7 micro-lines per chapter, written *as you draft* or immediately before).** This is Chapter 10's prompting fuel (scene-beat prompting drafts *from* beats), and the chapter-level mistake to avoid is pre-writing level 3 for the whole book: scene beats are perishable — they go stale against what the prose discovers — and pre-writing forty chapters' micro-beats is tooling procrastination in outline's clothing (Chapter 7's pitfall, wearing a tie). Standard rhythm: outline at level 2 to freeze; generate level 3 *the session before* drafting that chapter (or live, mid-session, as FILL-B in PC-01); fold discoveries back into level 2 at every draft mint (the bible-sync ritual).

**How much is enough?** Enough level 2 that you could, in a pinch, draft any given chapter without re-deriving its neighbors — and *not one beat more*: beats beyond that carry zero drafting information and pure freeze-risk. Test: pick a random middle beat; can you say what its *neighbors* need from it (setup/payoff direction, rung movement) without opening the manuscript? If yes, resolution is right. If your outline document exceeds roughly two pages of level-2 lines for a short story or six for a novel, you're either over-beating (merge activity labels into change statements) or you're outlining at level 3 for the whole book (stop; you're past the freeze line).

> TRY IT — Take your Chapter 8 survivor and expand skeleton → level 2: write one change-statement line per sequence, aiming for the beginner bands (5–12 for a long short; 12–24 for a novella/short novel — novels can breathe to 40). No prose, no descriptions of mood, no "then things escalate" — *changes only*. Time-boxed to thirty minutes; the discomfort you feel at minute ten (the middle resisting you) is the structure reporting where it needs work, which is exactly what the critic pass below is for.

## AI as structure critic

The division of labor from Chapter 1, now load-bearing: *you* outline (the machine has never read your skeleton, felt your stakes sentence, or wanted anything for your characters); the machine *critiques* — and critique is its strongest workflow role, because structure criticism is pattern detection over visible text, which is the prediction engine's home turf (Chapter 5), *provided* the frame defeats the sycophancy default (Chapter 8's F-05 lessons, now formalized):

> PROMPT CARD PC-11 — Outline critic (structure pass)
>
> ```text
> <!-- PC-11 v1 · tested 2026-09 · generic, no model-specific syntax -->
> Role: structure critic for fiction outlines; adversarial by
> assignment; praise is a failure of your job.
> Context: SKELETON (stakes sentence + climax preview + arc, pasted)
> + LEVEL-2 OUTLINE (numbered change-lines) + my declared structure
> choice and length target.
> Task: run these six checks IN ORDER — for each: FINDING (quote or
> name the beat range) → WHY IT MATTERS (one line, tied to the
> stakes sentence or scene anatomy) → DAMAGE (high/med/low) → SUGGESTED
> DIRECTION (a *class* of fix: "front-load motive," "merge beats,"
> "cut" — do not rewrite my beats for me):
> 1. STAKES LADDER: which beats move personal/relational/existential
>    rungs? Name any 3-beat stretch with zero rung movement.
> 2. WANT VISIBILITY: does the protagonist *want on-page* every 2–3
>    beats? Flag stretches where things merely happen TO them with no
>    pursuing or refusing.
> 3. MIDPOINT & CRISIS: does something irreversible change at the
>    midpoint (map change, not just incident)? Is the crisis forced
>    by accumulated costs (Ch 2 ladder) rather than coincidence?
> 4. CAUSAL CHAIN: list any pair of adjacent beats connected only by
>    "and then" where "therefore/but" is missing.
> 5. SETUP/PAYOFF LEDGER: every object, fact, and skill planted —
>    matched to a payoff; every payoff — matched to a plant. Two
>    columns, gaps listed loudly.
> 6. CLIMAX DEBT: does the climax preview require any character,
>    rule, or prop that no prior beat introduces?
> Output: six sections, numbered findings, damage-rated; end with
> the THREE highest-damage findings only. No summary paragraph. No
> praise. No rewritten outline.
> ```

Every design line in that card has a failure it prevents: *pasted skeleton* grounds the critique in *your* aims (otherwise the critic evaluates against generic good-outline-ness — and your deliberate kishōtenketsu twist gets flagged as "under-conflicted"); *classes of fix, not rewrites* prevents the machine from becoming the outline's real author (and your adjudication collapsing into rubber-stamping — the authorship trap of Chapter 15's spirit, caught early); *damage ratings* force prioritization (twelve equal notes are noise — Chapter 13's triage lesson, pre-installed); *three-findings close* beats recency bias (the final three notes are the ones you'll remember — make them the worst three); *no summary paragraph* starves the agreeable wind-down ("overall, a strong outline with minor opportunities…") before it starts.

**The adjudication ritual — non-negotiable, the chapter's spine.** After every critic pass: open the decisions file (or an outline-notes file hanging off it) and log, *for every finding*: kept / rejected / deferred, one line of reason. Example real rows from Example A's first pass (you'll see these fully in the worked example): *"- PC-11 #3 midpoint was incident not map-change → KEPT: journal-reveal now changes Theo's leverage (stakes line tie-in)." / "- PC-11 #6 suggested adding a courtroom beat for climax prop → REJECTED: length target + we refuse legal-drama genre creep (bible §5)."* The ritual's mechanics matter: findings you can't find in your outline = you and the critic read different documents (go re-read before deciding — misfiring notes reveal outline ambiguities, which are *findings about findings* worth a v-next edit); findings that are right but expensive get DEFERRED with a revisit date (deferral with a date is intelligence; deferral without one is deletion with extra steps). Run the whole pass *before* drafting level 3 anywhere — structure corrections are free at level 2 and expensive at level 3, the Chapter 2 law at another altitude.

### When discovery writers meet outlines

Part of this book's readership drafts by discovering, and hears "beat outline" as a cage. The honest reconciliation, since the workflow needs a level-2 freeze to feed Chapters 10–12: **the outline you commit to can be written *after* a discovery draft, and many do it that way.** Path A (planners): skeleton → level 2 → critic loop → draft from beats. Path B (discoverers): skeleton (you already have one — even discovery writers keep a compass) → draft *one scene at a time*, checking only that the scene's goal/conflict/outcome serves the skeleton's five fields → *reverse-outline* (write the level-2 line for each scene as completed, in past tense: "Mara arrives; keys force her to find Theo first") → run the critic pass on the *reverse* outline before revising (Chapter 12) rather than before drafting. Both paths converge on the same artifact — a level-2 outline that has survived one adversarial pass — before any structural revision begins; they differ only in *when the lines were written*. The pitfall below (outline worship) applies with equal force to both: a Path-B writer who *refuses* to look at their reverse outline before revision is not discovering; they're postponing the diagnosis.

## Pacing and subplots

Two instruments beyond the six checks, each with a card, each earning its keep at specific moments (critic pass #1 for the six checks; these two when the six checks come back *clean* but the read still feels off — pacing and weave are the finer gauges).

> PROMPT CARD PC-12 — Pacing checker
>
> ```text
> <!-- PC-12 v1 · tested 2026-09 · generic -->
> Role: pacing analyst; you measure tempo, you don't judge ideas.
> Input: LEVEL-2 OUTLINE with each beat's approximate word-share
> estimate (my numbers, my responsibility) OR chapter word-targets
> if drafting has begun.
> Task: (1) Build a tempo table: beat | space allotted | pressure type
> (pursuit/refusal/revelation/reaction/transition) | rung moved
> (P/R/E/—). (2) Flag: any 3+ consecutive 'transition' or '—' rows;
> any revelation cluster within 2 beats (informational indigestion);
> any rung-movement gaps longer than 4 rows. (3) Compare my
> midpoint's share of total space to my structure's teaching
> default (three-act ≈ 40–50%; note deviations without correcting
> them). Output: table, then flags, then stop — no recommendations
> beyond flag classes.
> ```

Pacing, operationalized: **space + pressure-type distribution + rung rhythm**, not "feels slow." The tempo table turns the felt problem into a visible one (the Act II hole is three transition rows and two "—"s in a row — you can *see* the corridor the hook's reader walked), and the flags-without-recommendations rule keeps PC-12 an instrument rather than a second critic (recommendation authority stays consolidated in PC-11's adjudication — two cards prescribing fixes will disagree, and split prescriptions are how outlines fork into paralysis). Space estimates are *yours* by design ("my numbers, my responsibility") — estimates forced by you are honest approximations; estimates the machine invents are fiction about fiction.

> PROMPT CARD PC-13 — Subplot weaver
>
> ```text
> <!-- PC-13 v1 · tested 2026-09 · generic -->
> Role: subplot engineer; every subplot must pay rent in the main
> line or leave the document.
> Context: LEVEL-2 OUTLINE (main line) + my subplot inventory below
> (name · thematic echo of main want · structural function).
> Task: for EACH subplot: (1) ENTRY: name the earliest beat where
> its absence would be noticed — if none, mark VESTIGIAL; (2) TOUCH
> BEATS: every main-line beat where the subplot must surface (min
> every 4–5 beats — flag longer silences); (3) PAYOFF: the exact
> beat where it changes stakes or meaning of the main line — if you
> can't point at one, mark RENT-FREE and recommend parking; (4)
> COLLISION: the beat where subplot and main line *physically share
> a scene* (thematically adjacent subplots with no shared scene are
> two stories mailing each other). Output: per-subplot cards +
> inventory of VESTIGIAL / RENT-FREE items. I decide what to park.
> ```

The four marks (entry / touch / payoff / collision) are the whole weaving discipline compressed: subplots die of *neglect* (forgotten for twelve beats — the reader forgets too, and re-entry costs a scene of re-introduction) and of *delusion* (thematic resonance mistaken for structural function — "it echoes the inheritance theme" is not rent; a beat that *changes a main-line decision* is rent). The inventory preamble forces *you* to have named functions before the machine audits them — again the pattern: you propose aims, it tests structure against them. Budget guidance for beginners: **one subplot per ~15 level-2 beats in a novel, zero subplots in a long short story** unless the form is braided on purpose (a short story with a vestigial subplot is almost always a novella that won't admit it — the capacity check from Chapter 8 catching a scale error from the other side).

**When to abandon the outline.** The escape hatch, stated without romance: you abandon — not "bend," *abandon and rebuild* — when (1) the critic loop can't converge: three passes producing *new* high-damage findings rather than converging (the F-07 signature at outline stage — usually means the skeleton's fields 4/5/10 were soft all along: go *up* a level, fix the stakes or obstacle, regenerate level 2 wholesale rather than patching); (2) drafting has *disproved* a load-bearing beat (your hands know something your outline didn't — the discovery-draft or mid-draft disproof is information, not betrayal); (3) the book you're actually writing has different *aims* than the one you outlined (premise drift — return to Chapter 8's logline first: if the logline is still true, the outline betrayed it; if the logline isn't true anymore, the premise stage reopens). The mechanics of abandonment are boring and vital: **mint nothing mid-panic** — write `outline-v2 ABANDONED · reason` in the changelog, re-derive from the skeleton (+ bible syncs), re-run PC-11 on the fresh v-next. What you never do: silently rewrite outline beats inside the doc (un-changelog'd structure edits are how "felt fine until Act II" holes get *re-seeded* — you'll fight this same hole at draft stage with no memory of changing the conditions). And the counter-hatchet: **an outline is never abandoned for the crime of being followed** — if the draft proceeds as planned, that's not rigidity, that's an outline working; discovery-culture's suspicion of plans and planner-culture's worship of them are the same error mirrored — the document serves the story, and the changelog is how you keep *that* relationship honest too.

### Level-2 line clinic: change-lines before and after

The granularity rule ("changes, not activity") is easiest to install by *editing* — specimen lines, diagnosed in the order their failures usually appear:

| ❌ Activity label | ❌ Scene description | ✅ Change-line |
|---|---|---|
| "Mara's arrival scene" | "Mara drives up in the rain and enters the house" | "Mara arrives expecting an empty house — finds Theo's boxes; the sale is now a standoff" |
| "The storm hits" | "Dramatic storm sequence with power outage" | "Storm cuts power *and* both exit routes — the clock stops being calendar and becomes physical" |
| "Mara and Theo talk" | "A long conversation about their mother" | "Theo names the promise he made; Mara's exit plan loses its moral cover" |

Read down the right column aloud: every line contains a *before → after* the reader can name (standoff discovered; routes gone; moral cover lost) — the Chapter 4 scene-anatomy contract lifted to structure level: beats *promise* scenes that change something, and the promise is testable before drafting. The left column's sins, named: **activity label** (no opinion about what's at stake — a table of contents for your own scenes, useless under pressure), **scene description** (previews prose mood instead of committing to change — the outline starts *writing for you*, which is how beat-lists ossify into must-imitate scripts), and — the subtle third — **backstory recap** ("Mara remembers the argument of '09" filed as a beat: memory is *material inside* a beat; it only earns a line when the *remembering changes a present decision*).

Editing drill (feeds the Try exercise): take five activity lines from any outline you've written and convert them right-column-style — the test for done is the *neighbor test*: with only your five lines visible, can you say how each change *pressures* the next? ("standoff discovered" → wants-a-clean-exit impossible → notary-clock *matters*) — activity lines don't pressurize neighbors; change-lines do, and the pressure *is* the outline's real content. When PC-11's check #4 flags "and then" pairs, the cure is almost always this clinic: beats that don't *change* anything can't be linked by "therefore/but," because there's no state for the conjunction to bite on.

### Critique-pass hygiene: contexts, batching, fresh sessions

Three operational rules that keep PC-11 (and its siblings PC-20…22 in Chapter 12) producing signal:

**Fresh session per pass, always.** A critic run inherits every prior exchange's gravity — the model has been *your drafting partner* for forty messages; its "critique" will now negotiate with the tone it learned from you (sycophancy's most sophisticated form: not flattery, but *continuity of agreeableness*). Passes are verdicts, not conversations: new session, full context paste (skeleton + outline + card), zero warm-up. The mild cost of re-pasting is the price of an unanchored reader.

**Context packs, assembled from the hub.** Never paste from memory's idea of the documents; paste from files: bible §1 + stakes + the frozen outline + length/structure declarations — a standard "context pack" of 300–600 words you can re-assemble in a minute *because the bible's sections already have the headings*. Chapter 7's hub-and-spoke paying off at altitude: passes that used to require twenty minutes of rummaging become a copy-ritual, which is why the pass actually gets run instead of skipped when tired (compliance with your own process is a design problem — design for tired-you).

**Batch by judgment type, not by anxiety.** The temptation: run a new pass every time a doubt surfaces mid-draft ("is it the midpoint? is it pacing? — let me ask"). Doomed: doubts compound into overlapping passes whose findings interleave, and adjudication rows start contradicting each other (you kept #3 from pass A, rejected #2 from pass B, and can't now tell which outline version either read). The discipline: **one pass type per outline version, ordered** (six-check structure first → pacing table second → weaving third — each *after* the previous adjudications have been applied and re-minted), doubts collected in the session log's blockers column between passes ("note: pacing suspicion — queued for PC-12 after structure v-next"). Findings against *stale* documents are worse than no findings: they send you editing beats a kept row already moved. Version discipline for passes: every critic prompt's paste carries the outline's version header (`outline-v3`), and adjudication rows open with the same version tag — if a row can't name its target, the row is invalid and the pass gets re-run against current state. It reads bureaucratic; it is the difference between a loop that converges in two passes and one that *vibrates* (F-07's outline-stage sibling — the convergence signature you'll meet formally in Chapter 12).

### The tempo table, run on Example A (PC-12 live)

Instruments teach best with readings taken, so here is PC-12's output on the v1 outline from the worked example — flags first, adjudications after (the card *forbids* recommendations, which is exactly why the rows below are *yours* to write):

```text
beat | space(est %) | pressure type | rung
1 arrival/keys      | 6  | orientation→refusal      | P?
2 notary+Casi       | 8  | revelation (clock starts)| P
3 porch standoff    | 9  | refusal/refusal          | — ←flag r1
4 storm warning     | 5  | pursuit (self-set deadline)| P
5 kitchen fight     | 11 | revelation + refusal     | R ✓
6 journal found     | 8  | revelation               | R
7 Aurelia coffee    | 7  | revelation               | E ←flag r2
   (transition-ish entry — see adjudication)
8 power+Theo reveal | 10 | revelation + revelation  | E? ←flag r3
9 Casi contract     | 9  | pursuit                  | — ←flag r4
10 night journal    | 12 | revelation               | E
11 dawn signing     | 14 | refusal/decision         | P/R

```

*Flags, ranked:* **r3 (revelation cluster, beats 6–8):** three information-beats in a row — informational indigestion *in the outline itself* (readers of the draft would receive the journal, the neighbor, and the promise as a *lecture series* unless something between them *presses*); **r5 (midpoint share low):** corroborates r3's diagnosis from the other instrument — the middle isn't just *dense with news*, it's *under-weighted in space*, which is how "felt fine until Act II" holes get built from *too much plot in too little room* dressed as the opposite problem; **r1 + r4 (rung gaps around refusal/pursuit beats):** beats 3 and 9 are *behaviorally right* (standoff, escalation) but *structurally silent* on rung movement — they *enact* conflict without *advancing stakes*, which at level 2 means their change-lines under-specified (the Ch 9 clinic's conversion will fix them: beat 3's line must *state* what the standoff costs Theo's keeping-plan, not just note mutual ignoring); **r2 (Aurelia as transition):** likely *healthy* literary breathing (the adjudication from the worked example already treated this adjacency as mood-chained by design) — flagged-but-kept, consistent with the v1 ritual's rejected-with-craft-substitute row.

*The adjudications' shape* (writing them out is the exercise's real content): r3+r5 → **KEPT together** — the fix that serves both: a *press-beat* inserted between 6 and 7 (Mara attempts the notary call — refused for information she now can't use — the pursuit flicker *and* a revelation-breaker in one line — note this is the *same* micro-pursuit the PC-11 ritual already queued as the 6b insert: **two independent instruments recommending the same fix class = the chapter's corroboration pattern working again**); r1/r4 → **DEFERRED to level-3 drafting** with dates ("when beat 3's scene is written, its change-line must name the cost — verify at mint"), the noted-typo altitude rule applied to *outline quality itself*; r2 → **WAIVED-with-reason** citing the literary-register note (the waiver row *then* gets pasted into any future PC-12 re-run's context — "known waivers: beat 7 adjacency" — so the instrument stops re-spending its flag-budget on settled weather). The tempo table's deeper lesson in one line: **flags are cheap, verdicts are the product** — an instrument that flags and *stops* leaves you with a prettier spreadsheet; an instrument whose flags pass the adjudication ritual leaves you with *an outline that has been measured, argued with, and versioned* — which is the only kind of outline that deserves the word "frozen" in its changelog entry.

### Outline maintenance during drafting: the living document

"The outline serves the draft" needs a *mechanics* section, or it decays into good intentions — so here is the maintenance regime that keeps `outline-vN` and `draft-current` speaking to each other without either dominating, written as the week-by-week habits it actually is:

**At every mint (the two-way sync, five minutes):** (1) *forward pass* — for each scene since the last mint, confirm its level-2 line exists and matches what got drafted (Path B writers *write* the line here — past-tense reverse-outline); (2) *back pass* — for each kept line, confirm the prose delivered it (a kept line undelivered = a *scene-pass queue row*, not a silent gap — the diff runs both directions or it runs *neither*, which is how "outline says one thing, draft says another" survives to beta readers); (3) *discovery capture* — anything the draft discovered that *changes structure* gets a v-next mint *the same session* (the two-rule stand's clock: divergence logged within one mint of birth — divergence older than one mint is how fix-echo starts, because the *next* critic pass reads the *document* and flags what the *prose* already knows); (4) *waiver & flag maintenance* — completed DEFERRED rows close (their level-3 fixes verified), open ones roll forward with dates intact; (5) *the changelog line* ties it all (outline-v-next referenced whenever prose-driven, prose mint referenced whenever outline-driven — the document pair keeps *each other's* receipts).

**Weekly during active drafting (ten minutes, Sunday's rhythm-audit slot):** skim *open threads + setup/payoff ledger columns* against what the week's scenes actually planted — the cheapest place to catch "payoff beam scheduled for ch11 with *no plant ever written*" is the week the plant was *due*, not the month the payoff was drafted; re-check beat-order assumptions that the week's rewrites quietly violated (the draft moves *scenes* mid-week more often than writers admit — the outline hears about it Sunday or not at all); and — the habit that keeps the document *alive rather than sacred* — one line in the audit: "outline current? yes/no + what's stale," because a stale outline in a folder is worse than none (it *invites* outline-worship from readers of it — including future-you skimming during a foggy Monday, ready to obey a map the territory has left behind).

**When the draft outruns the plan wholesale** (discovery-heavy stretches, a month where reverse-outlines dominate forward ones): the regime's escape valve — *declare an outline-pause* in the changelog ("v-next lags prose by design until month-end consolidation"), then run a **consolidation session** (90 minutes, at month-end, non-negotiable date): reverse-outline everything current, run PC-11 once on the *consolidated* document (fresh session, waivers pasted — the critic reads *one* state, not a month of drift), adjudicate, mint the reconciled v-next, clear the pause row. The consolidation pattern's deep purpose is *emotional as much as structural*: discovery writers need *permission* to let prose lead (the pause grants it in writing — no guilt-accumulation), and planners need *proof* the document will catch up (the scheduled session is that proof — both temperaments served by one mechanism because both are served by the same *thing*: **divergence with a clock and a reconciliation date is process; divergence without either is the F-03 family's nursery.** The living document, finally stated as a single sentence: *the outline is current as of the last mint, honest as of the last sync, and paused only where its pause is written down* — anything else and you're maintaining a map of a town you've already left.

## Worked example: Example A outline v1 → critique → v3

The full loop on your running example — condensed where the method repeats what's printed above, expanded where adjudications teach.

**v1 (author-written from the skeleton; level 2, long-short band — 11 beats):**

```text
OUTLINE v1 — "Low Tide" (from skeleton v1; structure: three-act)

1.  Mara arrives; keys, house smell, half-packed kitchen; Theo
    won't meet her eyes. [act open — defining situation: the sale]
2.  Notary letter surfaced: both signatures by month's end; Casi
    appears charming, offers cash above value. [disturbance]
3.  Mara inventories rooms; Theo repairs the porch rail instead of
    packing — silent standoff across the house. [want visible:
    sell / resist]
4.  The storm warning; ferry cancelled; Mara decides to finish
    before weather traps them. [clock starts]
5.  Kitchen fight: December line lands; Mara retreats to the
    guest room. [first relational rung move]
6.  Mara finds the tide journal while "inventorying"; entries mix
    weather with Theo-visits she never heard about. [discovery —
    her want starts costing]
7.  Morning: Aurelia brings coffee; confirms the third place at
    the table, years of it. [relational/existential pressure]
8.  Power dies; flashlight kitchen; Theo explains why he kept
    maintaining the house (mother's project — he promised). [midpoint:
    information turns]
9.  Casi arrives early with contract; pressure peaks; Mara asks
    for a day. [opposition escalates]
10. Night: Mara reads the last journal entries; mother's last
    week; the unanswered calls in her own handwriting of dates.
    [crisis — costs fully visible]
11. Dawn: Casi returns; Mara signs or doesn't, with Theo watching
    from the doorway. [climax preview kept from skeleton]
```

**PC-11 pass (abridged — six checks, findings damage-rated; three highest close the card):**

> 1. STAKES LADDER: beats 3–5 stretch: one rung move (5) across three beats; beats 8–9 are reaction + escalation without rung movement. DAMAGE: med.
> 2. WANT VISIBILITY: Mara's want visible 1,2,4,9 — gap at 6–8 where *discovery* replaces *pursuing* (she stops acting on the sale for three beats). DAMAGE: med.
> 3. MIDPOINT: beat 8 changes *information* only; the map (who holds leverage over whom) does not flip — mother's promise HELPS Theo but nothing in Mara's options or constraints visibly changes. DAMAGE: **high**.
> 4. CAUSAL CHAIN: 7→8 "and then" (Aurelia's visit does not *cause* the power-dout conversation); 8→9 "and then". DAMAGE: med.

## Exercises

- **Try (15 min):** Beat out one chapter of your story: from your level-2 line (or skeleton field for a short piece), write 5–9 level-3 scene beats as *change-lines* ("she opens the letter; the date doesn't exist" — not "she reads"), then circle which rung each moves (P/R/E/—). Three beats showing "—" is legal in one chapter; *zero* rung movement across all of them means the chapter is a transition wearing a scene's clothes — flag it, don't fix it yet (the critic pass gets the last word next exercise).

- **Stretch (1–2 h):** Full loop on your story: level-2 outline drafted (bands from the granularity section), PC-11 run *once* in a fresh session with your pasted skeleton, then the adjudication ritual — every finding a decisions row (kept/rejected/partial/deferred, one-line reason each), kept findings applied by hand, `outline-v1` frozen, changelog line written. Budget: ~45 min outlining, ~20 min critique, ~45 min adjudication+edits. If the pass returns zero findings across six checks, your prompt didn't paste the skeleton (check the card's context line — a critic without *your* stakes sentence grades against the average outline, which is how "fine" happens).

- **Project (ongoing):** Freeze `outline-v1` (or v3, at whatever you reach) — from this moment, **all structure changes route through the changelog** (new mint, reason, PC-11 ref if re-critiqued). Habit paired with drafting: as scenes complete (Path B or level-3 folding), write the reverse/level-2 line immediately — the outline stays *current* against the manuscript, and Chapter 12's continuity critic inherits a structure doc that doesn't lie about the draft.

## Pitfalls

> PITFALL — **Outline worship.** *Symptom:* the draft's discovery — a character wants something the beats didn't predict, a scene reveals a better midpoint — gets *forced back* into the outline ("I'll make the prose disguise that beat 8 isn't a map-change after all"); or the inverse paralysis: the outline is abandoned at the first sign of divergence, draft and document drifting apart until revision has no map at all (the F-03 family: draft contradicts outline — *both* symptoms are the same error: the document treated as either holy writ or disposable scribble). *Why it happens:* sunk cost (forty-one beats feel like property) plus a misunderstanding of what the document *is* — a compression of decisions meant to serve scenes, which are where story actually happens; AI outlines intensify it (a critic-formatted document *looks* official) and discovery drafts soften it into amnesia. *Fix:* the two-rule stand: (1) **the outline serves the draft's aims** — when the draft's discovery serves the skeleton's stakes/climax better than the outline's beat does, *amend the outline* (changelog line, reason citing the discovery — Path B does this by reflex); (2) **the draft does not silently contradict the outline** — when the draft *needs* structural change, change happens *on paper first* (v-next mint + reason), then the prose follows — "I'll sort the structure in revision" is how holes are born. The skeleton (not the outline) is the aims-holder: disagreements get arbitrated against *it* — stakes sentence, climax preview — not against the beat list's pride. *Prevention:* freezing discipline (v-lines, changelog) makes amendments *painless and visible*; running PC-11 before drafting makes the outline already *tested*, so worship and distrust both have less mystical material to attach to.

Related failure for Appendix C: **F-03 (draft contradicts outline)** — joint home with Ch 12; the fix there adds the draft-side audit (outline-vs-draft diff pass), this chapter's adds the change-routing rule; both serve one invariant: *documented, adjudicated divergence — or convergence, but never silent drift.*

## Key takeaways

- **Granularity is a ladder, not a dump:** skeleton-derived movements → level-2 change-lines (frozen after critique) → level-3 scene beats (written session-close to drafting). Enough to draft any chapter without re-deriving neighbors — never past the freeze line.
- **The machine critiques structure better than it outlines it:** PC-11's six checks are pattern-detection (its home turf), framed against *your* pasted skeleton, forbidden from rewriting beats, closed with a top-three — and every finding passes through the **adjudication ritual** (kept/rejected/partial/deferred + one-line reason) before touching the document.
- **Pacing and weaving are instruments:** PC-12's tempo table (space × pressure-type × rung rhythm) makes "feels slow" visible; PC-13's four marks (entry/touch/payoff/collision) charge every subplot rent — flags only; prescriptions stay consolidated in one adjudicated pass.
- **Abandoning is a procedure, not a mood:** three disproofs justify it; changelog-and-rebuild mechanics prevent re-seeded holes; and following an outline that's working is *not* the failure mode — silent divergence in either direction is.
- **Example A's loop is the template you just ran:** v1 hand-written → adversarial six-check pass → ritual rows (three kept, one craft-substituted, one partial, one dated deferral) → v3 diff → frozen with scars visible. Structure you can *read the tests of* is the outline that finally earns its afternoon of satisfaction.
