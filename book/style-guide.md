# Style Guide — Write with AI

## Audience & reading level

- Beginner fiction writers; many will be non-native English speakers.
- Plain language, short paragraphs, jargon always defined on first use.
- Encouraging but honest: never promise AI shortcuts that skip learning the craft.

## Voice & tone

- Second person ("you"), practical, warm, direct.
- Show the work: annotated examples beat assertions. Prefer transcripts-with-commentary
  over "AI can help you brainstorm."
- Calibrated confidence: "often / sometimes / rarely" over "always / never."
- No hype, no doom: AI is a tool with a specific shape — describe the shape.

## Chapter anatomy (required)

Every chapter contains, in this order:

1. **Opening hook** — a concrete scene, failure, or before/after (≤ 300 words).
2. **Learning objectives** — 3–5 bullets ("By the end you will be able to…").
3. **Body sections** — each section teaches one technique with an example.
4. **Worked example** — running Example A or B, or a self-contained demo.
5. **Exercises** — three tiers: `Try` (15 min) · `Stretch` (1–2 h) · `Project` (ongoing).
6. **Pitfalls** — what goes wrong and the fix (feeds Appendix C).
7. **Checklist** — "you're ready to move on when…".
8. **Key takeaways** — ≤ 5 bullets.
9. **Research notes** (manuscript only, not printed) — claims needing verification,
   with claim IDs pointing at `research/claim-register.md`.

## Callout types (use exactly these)

- `> TRY IT` — a small action to do right now.
- `> PROMPT CARD PC-XX` — a reusable prompt (also collected in Appendix A).
- `> PITFALL` — a common failure and its fix.
- `> CRAFT NOTE` — pure writing-craft aside (works with or without AI).
- `> REALITY CHECK` — where AI genuinely struggles; limits stated plainly.
- `> ETHICS NOTE` — only in Ch 13/15 unless truly necessary elsewhere.
- `> CHECKLIST` — end-of-chapter readiness checks.

## Prompts & transcripts

- Prompts in fenced code blocks, copy-paste ready, with a version line:
  `<!-- PC-07 v1 · tested 2026-09 · generic, no model-specific syntax -->`
- AI transcripts: abridged with `[...]`; every transcript gets 2–5 lines of
  **commentary** explaining *why* it worked or failed. Never print raw output uncommented.
- Prompts must be model-generic. Model-specific tricks go to the companion site, dated.

## Terminology (use consistently)

- **AI assistant** (the tool in a writing role), **model** (the underlying system),
  **prompt** (your instruction), **draft** (numbered: Draft 1, Draft 2…),
  **pass** (a revision pass), **story bible** (project reference doc).
- **Co-drafting**, **voice anchor**, **pass system**, **feedback-to-changes table** —
  book-specific terms, defined once, then used consistently (also in Glossary).

## Numbers, claims & tools

- Every factual claim (law, statistic, capability) needs a claim ID and a register row
  before the chapter leaves `draft` status. No exceptions for "everybody knows."
- Statistics keep their units, population, and date — or get cut.
- Tool references are generic-first ("your AI assistant"); named products only in dated
  asides, never load-bearing for a technique.
- Legal/regulatory content: official source or it doesn't ship; frame as information,
  not legal advice.

## Inclusivity & examples

- Character names and scenarios across a range of cultures; avoid stereotypes, including
  "default" ones. Vary genders, ages, settings across chapters.
- Content warnings where an example touches violence, abuse, or similar — one line suffices.

## AI transparency (we teach it, we do it)

- Manuscript passages substantially drafted with AI assistance are logged for Appendix F
  (chapter, section, tool role, human review). The log is part of the book's argument.
- No AI-generated passage ships without human revision and a logged review.
